Space Chase: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Space Chase is a single-player game with an optional online leaderboard for the daily challenges. The only thing that ever leaves your device is what you choose to put on the leaderboard: a nickname and your scores.

What the game stores on your device

Your scores, today's best run (the "ghost"), your day streak, your settings and the leaderboard nickname you picked are stored on your device. Uninstalling the app (or clearing the site's data in your browser) deletes them.

The leaderboard

The main menu and the end of each daily run show today's leaderboard. To get it, the game asks the leaderboard server for today's top scores. That request carries no personal data.

The leaderboard is opt-in. Only if you type a nickname to join it does the game send, after each daily run:

  • the nickname you typed,
  • which daily challenge it was and the date,
  • your best score in that challenge that day.

Your nickname and scores are public: anyone who plays can see them. Pick a nickname, not your real name. Changing your nickname moves today's scores to the new one.

The leaderboard runs on Cloudflare. Like any server on the internet, it sees the IP address a request comes from in order to answer it. The game does not store IP addresses or link them to nicknames. Scores are kept on the server; to have a nickname and its scores removed, email the address below.

What the game does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, email addresses or real names.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or advertising identifiers.
  • No network requests while you fly. The game works offline; without a connection the leaderboard simply doesn't show.
  • No access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone or files.

Sharing

If you tap Share after a run, the game hands a short line of text (your score and the date) to your device's share menu or clipboard. You choose where it goes. The game itself sends nothing.

Children

The game asks no one, including children, for personal information. The leaderboard nickname is shown to everyone, so children should pick one that isn't their real name, or not join the leaderboard.

Changes

If what the game sends ever changes, this page will be updated before that version is released.
